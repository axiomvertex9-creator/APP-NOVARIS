LEARN THE SCIENCE

01 · What is a black hole?

A region of spacetime enclosed by an event horizon, beyond which no light or other signal can reach distant observers. Some form when massive stars collapse.

02 · How can it pull light?

Light has no rest mass, but gravity in general relativity is the curvature of spacetime. Photons follow paths called null geodesics, which curve near massive bodies.

03 · Why can't light escape?

Inside the event horizon, all future-directed light paths remain inside. Light still moves locally at c: it is spacetime geometry, not ordinary friction, that prevents escape.

04 · What is the event horizon?

The horizon is a one-way causal boundary, not a hard surface. For a non-spinning black hole, its radius is rₛ = 2GM/c², where G is the gravitational constant, M is mass, and c is light speed.

05 · Why does the disk glow?

Orbiting gas heats as it compresses and experiences turbulence, emitting radiation. The bright disk belongs to nearby material, not the black hole itself.

06 · What is gravitational lensing?

Curved spacetime deflects incoming light, making distant objects appear shifted, distorted, or magnified. The glow and lensing in this app are visual approximations.

07 · Can planets orbit safely?

Yes. Far from the horizon, a black hole can have ordinary gravitational orbits. A stable orbit depends on distance, velocity and black hole properties.

08 · Is this exact relativity?

No. Matter uses a simplified central-force model; photon paths use a visually exaggerated deflection model. The disk, lensing and spacetime grid are artistic teaching aids, not numerical solutions of Einstein's field equations.
